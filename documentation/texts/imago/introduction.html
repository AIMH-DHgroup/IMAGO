The IMAGO Ontology was developed to support the semantic modelling of manuscripts and printed editions from the Middle Ages and Renaissance, particularly those addressed within the IMAGO project. These materials present complex bibliographic, codicological, and iconographic characteristics that require a nuanced and interoperable representation framework.

Traditional cataloguing approaches often separate textual, material, editorial, and contextual aspects of historical resources. The IMAGO Ontology addresses this fragmentation by integrating the intellectual dimension of works (expressions and genres), the material dimension of manuscripts and printed editions, the agentive dimension (authors, publishers, curators), the geographical dimension (places and toponyms), and the paratextual and iconographic dimension (incipits, explicits, dedications, iconographic apparatus).

Built according to the Methontology methodology, the ontology follows a structured engineering lifecycle including specification, conceptualisation, formalisation, implementation, evaluation, and maintenance. It adopts CIDOC CRM and FRBRoo/LRMoo as foundational ontological frameworks to ensure compatibility with established cultural heritage standards, while introducing domain-specific extensions tailored to the description of IMAGO resources.

Particular attention is given to the modelling of manuscript folios and custodial institutions, the semantic representation of editorial responsibility (curators, publishers), the identification and contextualisation of places through toponyms, the encoding of scholarly annotations and documentary sources, and the linkage between physical objects and their digital representations.

The IMAGO Ontology thus provides a coherent, extensible, and standards-aligned semantic infrastructure for the study, preservation, and digital integration of medieval and Renaissance documentary and visual heritage. It enables consistent data modelling and facilitates interoperability, linked data publication, and advanced scholarly research in the digital humanities domain.
