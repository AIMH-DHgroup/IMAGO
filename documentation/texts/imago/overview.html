The IMAGO Ontology provides a formal semantic framework for representing bibliographic, codicological, iconographic, and editorial information related to medieval and Renaissance manuscripts and printed editions. It is published under a Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial 4.0 International license and maintained by ISTI-CNR within the IMAGO project.

Conceptual Foundations

The ontology is grounded in and interoperable with:

  • CIDOC CRM (Erlangen CRM implementation) for cultural heritage documentation.
  • FRBRoo/LRMoo (ILRMoo namespace) for modelling works, expressions, manifestations, and items.
  • FOAF, Dublin Core Terms, PROV-O, DOAP, and VANN for metadata, provenance, and repository description.

By extending these standards, IMAGO ensures semantic interoperability while addressing domain-specific requirements not fully covered by existing ontologies.

Core Modelling Areas

The ontology models:

  1. Intellectual and Bibliographic Entities
    • Expressions (works) and their genres
    • Manifestations and printed editions
    • Manuscripts as physical items
    • Editions, formats, and typologies
  2. Actors and Roles
    • Authors (linked to expression creation)
    • Publishers and curators (linked to manifestation creation)
    • Libraries as custodial institutions
  3. Textual and Paratextual Elements
    • Incipits and explicits (textual and dedication-related)
    • Symbolic objects with textual content
    • Introduction notes, figure notes, iconographic apparatus
  4. Places and Toponyms
    • Geographic entities (E53 Place)
    • Toponyms as specialized appellations
    • Printed-edition-specific place names
    • Textual references to places within works
  5. Scholarly and Documentary Annotations
    • Primary and secondary sources
    • Reprint dates and editorial notes
    • Links to digital manuscript representations and descriptions

Intended Audience

The ontology is designed for researchers in medieval and Renaissance studies, digital humanities scholars, cultural heritage institutions, and data curators and semantic web developers. It supports structured description, interoperability, linked open data publication, and advanced knowledge representation for scholarly analysis.
