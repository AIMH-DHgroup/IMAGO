The ontology organizes bibliographic and cultural heritage knowledge around a small set of foundational categories: intellectual products, events/processes, agents, and identification/terminology. The model is designed to be used in conjunction with CIDOC CRM-aligned descriptions, enabling cross-domain integration without sacrificing bibliographic specificity.

  • Intellectual products and their levels of abstraction: the ontology supports the differentiation between conceptual creations (e.g., Works), their realizations (e.g., Expressions), their embodiments (e.g., Manifestations), and their individual exemplars (e.g., Items).
  • Lifecycle events: creation, production, publication, performance, recording, reproduction, and other activities are explicitly represented to capture provenance, responsibility, and temporal context.
  • Agents and roles: persons and collective entities can participate in events and be linked to intellectual products through well-defined relationships that clarify responsibility (e.g., creator, producer, performer, publisher).
  • Identification and terminology: entities can be associated with names, identifiers, and controlled vocabularies to support disambiguation and consistent referencing across datasets.

Implemented in OWL 2 DL, the ontology is suitable for reasoning-based applications such as entity consolidation, integrity checking via ontology-aware constraints, inference of implicit relations, and semantic search over bibliographic and heritage collections.
